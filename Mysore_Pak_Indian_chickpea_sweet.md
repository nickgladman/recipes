# Mysore Pak Recipe

* original recipe: https://www.indianhealthyrecipes.com/mysore-pak-recipe/#wprm-recipe-container-37352
* Author: Swasthi Shreekanth
* Swasthi's Recipes
* Modified by Nick

**A popular South Indian sweet made with gram flour, sugar and lots of ghee. This recipe yields Mysore pak that has a delicate crumb with a mouth melting texture**

Prep Time Cook Time Total Time
5 mins 25 mins 30 mins
* Servings: 12 pieces 

### Ingredients
1 cup besan or gram flour, sifted (120 grams)
  * This is also known as chickpea flour. I use Bob's Red Mill
1.75 cup sugar (can reduce to 1.25 cup but texture will be like burfi) (350 grams)
0.5 cup water
1 cup ghee (pure good quality ghee) (218 grams of solid ghee is more than enough)
0.5 cup oil (preferably peanut oil, if you skip it will be like burfi) (refer to original
notes)

### Instructions
**Preparation**

**order of prep: start ghee and oil on range, then sugar syrup on range, then sift the flour**

1. Pour 1 tbsp ghee to a small tray and grease it well for setting the
mysore pak. Set this aside.

2. Place a sieve over a large bowl. Measure and add besan to the
sieve.

3. Next sieve it well so there are no lumps. Divide the flour to 3 parts and transfer them
to small bowls. Set this aside.

4. On one burner of the stove place a kadai (or small saucepan) and pour ghee and oil.
Begin to heat it on a low to medium heat (I use setting 4-5 out of 10 for my electric range on the smaller burner).

### Make Sugar Syrup

1. On another burner, place a wide deep pan. Pour the sugar and water.
Begin to heat it. Use a medium-low temperature (4 setting out of 10 on electric)

2. Boil the sugar syrup stirring often until it reaches a one string
consistency. This is achieved at 220-222 Farenheit.

3. Alternatively, To check one string consistency, take a small portion of the syrup in a
spoon and cool down slightly. Take in between your thumb and index
finger. Move the fingers apart. You must be able to see a single string
in between the fingers. (check video)

4. Ensure the oil and ghee are turning hot. I use a small saucepan set to medium-high settings (4-5 out of 10 on an electric range)

### How to make Mysore Pak

1. Add 1/3 rd portion of flour to the bubbling sugar syrup. Flame/heat must be
medium and the sugar syrup bubbling well otherwise mysore pak
turns flat. Stir well until all the flour blends well with the syrup.

2. Then add the next 1/3 rd portion of flour. Repeat adding the last part
too following the same process.

3. At this stage there must be no lumps & the flour must blend well with
the sugar syrup.

4. Next add in 1 ladle full of hot ghee & oil to the pan. Then immediately
oil and ghee must sizzle, meaning it is hot enough. Make sure the
ghee is really very hot otherwise increase the flame of burner 1 to
keep ghee & oil consistently hot. Immediately stir well until all the
ghee has been absorbed.

5. Repeat adding the ghee in parts & repeat stirring until the ghee is well
absorbed every time.

6. As the ghee is added every time it has to sizzle. Then stir well until
absorbed. If you do not have enough ghee left in the kadai, then pour
2 tbsp more each of oil and ghee. Then heat it quickly. I did not use
any excess.

7. Quickly pour some hot ghee and stir. Do not add a lot at as it will
leave out the excess.

8. When the mysore pak is about to finish, the mixture will turn very thick
with lot of pores or bubbles. Do not add any more ghee at this stage
otherwise it will ooze out the excess. The mixture will leave the pan
completely within few seconds.

9. Be very very quick and transfer to a greased pan. Level the top with
the help of a greased spoon. Keep this aside for 10 to 15 mins. Invert
it on a board. Cut to desired sized pieces.

* Note: if you leave it for too long, it will become too brittle and not cut easily. I wouldn't wait longer than 10 mintues.
* Note: don't cut the pieces too thin either, otherwise they will break and you'll be left with a lot of small crumb pieces.

10. Cool mysore pak completely. Store in a air tight jar at room
temperature
